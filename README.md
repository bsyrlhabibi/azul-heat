Azul Heat: Two Runners is a top-down open-world crime game set in Bahía Azul, a fictional sunset coastal city of six districts: the Marina with its pier and beach, the terracotta lanes of Old Town, the neon-lit Strip, the pastel mansions of Palm Hills, dense Midtown, and the container yards of the Docks. You play two runners and can switch between them at any moment with Tab: Lucía Ferro, a courier whose hands make any car handle sweeter, and Marco Reyes, an ex-mechanic whose vehicles shrug off damage that would wreck anyone else's. Between them runs a six-mission story for three named contacts — Tía Carmen, El Contador, and DJ Prisma — dealt out through an in-game phone that buzzes with jobs, warnings, and gossip as the heat rises.

## What's in the city

- **A living open world** — two-way traffic that keeps to its lanes, pedestrians strolling the sidewalks, parked cars waiting to be borrowed, all of it washed in a golden-hour grade with long shadows, lit windows, neon signs bleeding light pools onto the pavement, street lamps pooling their own glow, an animated sparkling sea, the pier, dock cranes, and stacked shipping containers.
- **Steal and drive anything** — walk up to any car and take it with E. Driving comes with a handbrake for sliding turns, visible damage, and smoke that thickens as the bodywork gives up.
- **A five-star wanted system** — patrol cars and foot cops answer your crimes; at three stars the police throw up roadblocks, and at four a helicopter joins the hunt and pins you in its searchlight. Break line of sight or swap into a fresh car and the heat cools away.
- **Combat on foot** — mouse-aim shooting with health and armor to manage. Get wasted and you wake up at the hospital minus a respawn fee; get caught standing still near a cop and the bust meter fills instead.
- **Ways to earn and keep** — checkpoint street races and timed courier runs pay cash on the side; buy safehouses that double as save and garage points, build per-district reputation, and everything persists in localStorage saves.
- **A HUD that keeps you oriented** — a circular minimap with blips for cops, objectives, and safehouses, wanted stars, a speedometer when driving, district banners as you cross the city, the full city map on M, and the phone on T.
- **Sound from nothing** — procedural WebAudio synthesizes the engine, the sirens, the gunfire, and three switchable synth radio stations. There are no assets and no network: every pixel and every note is drawn or generated by the code itself.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| WASD / Arrows | Move & drive |
| Mouse / Click | Aim & shoot |
| E | Enter / steal car & interact |
| Space | Handbrake |
| Shift | Sprint |
| Tab | Switch runner |
| T | Phone |
| M | City map |
| P or Esc | Pause |
| H | Horn |
| 1 / 2 / 3 | Radio stations |
| N | Mute |

## Run it

The game is plain static files. Serve the game folder with any static server — for example `cd game && python3 -m http.server 8080` — and open it in a desktop browser.

The judged hand-in is produced with Node 22:

```
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

That copies `game/` and `screenshots/` into the output folder and writes `dossier.json`.

## Layout

- `main.ts` — the hand-in program: it assembles the output folder and the dossier.
- `vendor/makefun-game.ts` — the kit's dossier library, unchanged.
- `game/index.html` — loads the one-job modules in `game/src/`:
  - `world.js` builds the city; `traffic.js` and `pedestrians.js` fill it with life.
  - `vehicles.js` drives; `police.js` hunts; `characters.js` defines the two runners.
  - `missions.js` and `story.js` tell the story; `gigs.js` and `economy.js` pay for it.
  - `combat.js` fights; `phone.js` talks; `render.js` paints.
  - `hud.js` and `minimap.js` orient; `audio.js` synthesizes; `save.js` persists.
  - `shots.js` stages the screenshots in `screenshots/` — real renders of the running game, taken through the scenes named in `shots.json`.
